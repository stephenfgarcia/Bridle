# Judgments

Three judges scored every proposal from 1 to 10 on eight criteria (feasibility for a small team, live FEA latency, grading determinism and safety, extensibility for code packs, cost, security and tenancy, UX enablement, maintainability).

## Lens: Engineering lead of a 4-person team: buildability, sequencing, operational load (what can be down at 2 a.m. before a deadline), hiring reality (Rust + TypeScript + R3F + AWS on four people), whether the MVP ships in two quarters, and hidden complexity (components and seams the estimates do not pay for). Scores reflect ADR 0001 binding scope (R1-R18, guardrails: small fixed worker pool, no canary/previews, staging+production, 99.5%, per-tenant LLM counters + one global cap).

| proposal | feas | latency | determinism | packs | cost | security | UX | maint | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Keel: one core, two processes | 8 | 8 | 8 | 7 | 9 | 7 | 7 | 8 | **62** |
| Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first) | 6 | 8 | 9 | 8 | 6 | 9 | 8 | 7 | **61** |
| Keel | 5 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 7 | 8 | 8 | 6 | **58** |

Ranking: Keel: one core, two processes > Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first) > Keel

**Keel: one core, two processes:** Fewest things that can fail: one image as web + worker, Postgres as queue (pg-boss), outbox, audit index and store of record, S3, no Redis, no WebSockets, no second app framework, no Rust server runtime. The 125 engineer-week plan (about 9-10 months for four) is the only honest estimate but does not fit two quarters without cutting Diagnose, similarity and operator-console polish. Transactional enqueue (submission row + event + job in one commit) makes the zero-lost-submissions exit criterion a database invariant, which is the best safety property in any proposal. Weaknesses an eng lead must fix before pilot: the wasm engine runs on the pg-boss worker's Node event loop (no worker_threads or resource limits specified), so one pathological solve blocks the whole job processor and the burst math (8 parallel deterministic slots on two single-threaded processes with 4 vCPU) is off by 2-4x; Fargate tasks on public subnets with public IPs to dodge a NAT gateway; Auth.js plus hand-rolled TOTP/passkey code; 5 s polling for job and review status; Next.js route handlers as the entire API including /ops and the CLI; R9's 'checks library as its own pinned artifact' satisfied only on paper (same wasm bytes). Cost is the best (about $650-800/mo, no Redis/NAT, one Opus judge+narrative call at about $0.13/grade).

**Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first):** Strongest determinism and security story: Wasmtime runner with fuel/memory/wall limits and a module that imports nothing but memory, V8-versus-Wasmtime hash matrix in CI, replay bundles with a nightly replay sample, judge invocations stored verbatim with request/output hashes, only the judge service has internet egress, private subnets with VPC endpoints, schemars-generated schemas that remove TS/Rust drift. Live-loop budget is the most rigorous (stage-by-stage with CI gates) and SSE status streams are a cheap UX win. But it carries the most runtime surface for four people: Next.js SSR app plus a Fastify API (two frameworks, double auth/session/tracing plumbing), three ECS worker services, Redis/BullMQ plus Redis Streams and an outbox publisher process, a Rust runner subprocess with a CBOR stdio protocol, canon-node, cosign signing and nightly replay (both v1.x scope pulled forward). The proposal itself sizes the 105 engineer-weeks for a team of five; for four it is 8+ months. Per-grade LLM cost (about $0.17 median: separate narrative call, Opus on every route) misses the COST-02 $0.15 median at launch, and 'grading workers scale on queue depth' drifts from the ADR's small fixed pool.

**Keel:** Best extension seams: LoadsPack/ResistancePack traits so NSCP can reuse the AISC resistance module with its own loads pack, element and analysis registries, a real engine.wasm + checks.wasm split that actually satisfies R9 and makes exam mode a matter of never serving checks.wasm, packs/registry.json driving the task-builder picker, a partial-factor conformance fixture pack at MVP, data bundles loaded at runtime. Determinism is sound (same bytes in worker_threads with 1 GB cap and 60 s wall clock, judge cache). The hidden complexity is the problem: the 89 engineer-week estimate is not credible for the largest component list of the three (Fastify + Next, a WebSocket gateway with Redis fan-out, Redis Streams bus, two wasm artifacts to pin coherently, four registries, CellContext, IdentityProvider slots); workspace at 10 weeks, LMS at 7 and platform core at 6 are about 30% under the other proposals for identical scope, so the real number is 120+. WebSockets with presence and op streaming at MVP exceed the ADR (team presence/co-editing are v1.x). Reserved seams nobody exercises (fake second cell, dummy element kind, saml/lti slots, EventBus interface) are dead abstractions four engineers must keep green. Rules and judge stages share one 1 vCPU worker service, so LLM latency can starve deterministic evidence.

Fatal flaws:
- Keel: one core, two processes: The engine executes on the pg-boss worker's Node event loop with no worker_threads, memory cap or wall clock specified: one pathological or oversized solve freezes all queues and /healthz in that task, and the burst calculation (500 x 1.5 s / 8 slots) assumes 8 parallel deterministic slots that two single-threaded Node processes on 4 vCPU cannot provide. - Keel: one core, two processes: Fargate tasks on public subnets with public IPs (to avoid a NAT gateway) put the grading worker that holds learner data and the Anthropic key on the internet edge; this fails institutional security review expectations and the proposal's own ASVS posture. - Keel: one core, two processes: The honest 125 engineer-week plan is roughly 40% over two quarters for four engineers; shipping on the academic calendar requires either an explicit cut list now (Diagnose archetype, similarity job, operator-console polish, LLM record/replay extras) or a fifth engineer, and the proposal does not commit to either.
- Keel: one core, two processes: R9 (checks library versioned as its own pinned artifact) is satisfied only in the manifest: the checks bytes are the engine wasm, so every checks errata forces an engine re-pin and regrade decision across all tasks.
- Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first): Sized for five engineers, not four: 105 engineer-weeks spread across Next.js plus Fastify, three worker services, Redis/BullMQ plus Redis Streams, a Rust Wasmtime runner subprocess with a CBOR protocol, SSE fan-out, canon-node, cosign and nightly replay (v1.x scope pulled into MVP) is 8+ months for four, and none of the two-quarter math works.
- Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first): LLM cost is designed above target: a separate narrative call and Opus on every route give about $0.17 median per judged grade versus the COST-02 $0.15 median, with the fix deferred to effort tuning after the harness exists.
- Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first): Grading workers that 'scale on queue depth within a fixed ceiling' contradict the ADR guardrail of a priority queue with per-tenant caps and a small fixed worker pool, adding autoscaling policies to operate and tune during the first term.
- Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first): Two application frameworks (Next.js SSR app and a Fastify API) double the auth/session, CSRF, tracing and deploy plumbing and split the type graph, the opposite of what four people need.
- Keel: The 89 engineer-week estimate is not credible: workspace 10 weeks, LMS core 7 weeks and platform core 6 weeks are about 30% below the other two proposals for identical scope while the component list is the largest (Fastify + Next, WebSocket gateway with Redis fan-out, Redis Streams bus, two wasm artifacts, four registries, CellContext, IdentityProvider slots); the true number is 120+ and the schedule will blow up mid-term.

- Keel: Reserved-but-unexercised seams (CellContext with a fake second cell, dummy element kind in the registry, saml/lti IdentityProvider slots, EventBus interface, team fields and lock epochs) are dead abstractions four engineers must keep green; the proposal acknowledges they will drift.
- Keel: Rules and judge stages run in the same worker-grading service (1 vCPU / 2 GB), so LLM latency or a provider slowdown can starve deterministic checks and break the 15 s deterministic-evidence promise during a deadline burst.

LLM spend control is the tightest: one Opus route with judge+narrative in a single structured call (3-call cap honoured, about $0.13 median), global cap $2,000/month, per-tenant 80%/100% budgets, kill switch under 60 s with deterministic-only degradation and backfill, cache-share and 3x-spend anomaly alerts, provider concurrency 8 with jittered backoff. Observability (OTel, pino with redaction test, Sentry plus wasm panic hook, synthetic probes from two regions, enumerated alert set) and FERPA mechanics (disclosure events in the mutating transaction, hourly Object Lock copies, consent-token impersonation with denylist, per-tenant KMS-wrapped HMAC keys, 12-month encrypted LLM logs, DSR export/erasure/pseudonymization) are complete. Deductions on this lens: (1) SBX-02 is not met: the authoritative solve runs in-process inside the general worker (no worker_threads/terminate, no fuel or epoch limit) on Fargate tasks with public IPs and open egress, with no read-only root FS, non-root user or egress restriction stated, so one pathological model can stall a task carrying all 18 processor slots and half the fixed pool; (2) Auth.js v5's Credentials provider does not create database sessions (it is JWT-only unless the session row is hand-rolled in callbacks), so the design either loses SEC-02's 60-second revoke-all and the 2-hour admin idle timeout or quietly grows the custom auth code the other two proposals budget explicitly; (3) a bridle_ops database role for /ops implies an RLS-bypassing role reachable from the web process, which SEC-03 permits only for migrations; (4) deadline burst depends on an operator scaling 2 to 4 tasks and a global LLM concurrency of 8 (500 judge calls at about 30 s each is roughly 31 minutes, at the edge of the 95%-in-30-minutes target), while pg-boss polling, the outbox/audit partition writes and the grading writes share a db.t4g.medium with web traffic; a Postgres failover is simultaneously a web, queue and audit outage, which is acceptable at the 99.5% guardrail but is the one SPOF. No server-side Run fallback if the browser worker cannot run (latency 7). RPO 15 min / RTO 4 h met.

**Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first):** Best literal match to the must-level platform NFRs on this lens. Sandboxing: bridle-runner embeds Wasmtime with fuel, linear-memory (512 MB) and wall-clock (60 s) limits and runs as a subprocess in a read-only, non-root container with no egress except VPC endpoints; the wasm module imports only memory and a log sink; only worker-judge can reach the Anthropic API through NAT; data and workers sit in private subnets; images are cosign-signed and verified at deploy. Determinism is monitored rather than assumed: identical bytes under V8, SpiderMonkey, JavaScriptCore and Wasmtime asserted in CI, nightly replay of a sample of released grades, result_match distribution and resultHash mismatch treated as a paged system error. Audit is written in the same transaction with prevhash and shipped to Object Lock (7 years), llm-logs live under a dedicated KMS key for 12 months, learners can obtain their own disclosure log, DSR pseudonymization and legal-hold interplay are specified. Backup and recovery: PITR (RPO 15 min), 35-day daily and 1-year weekly snapshots copied to a second region, S3 cross-region replication for models, results, evidence and audit, secrets/KMS backups separate from primary storage, one rehearsed restore before the pilot (RTO 4 h), environment from IaC in 60 min. Burst: a separate judge lane with an adaptive limiter so provider latency never starves deterministic work, grading workers scale on queue depth within a fixed 2-6 ceiling, deterministic evidence visible within 15 s, 1-hour cache TTL inside deadline windows, deterministic-only degradation with oldest-first backfill, k6 burst test before every minor release. Deductions: the most components for a 3-5 person team (Next.js + Fastify + three worker services + runner binary + ElastiCache Redis + RDS Proxy + NAT + about ten S3 buckets + Grafana Cloud/Sentry/status page) on the lowest engineer-week estimate (105), so on-call surface and schedule pressure are the real risks; Redis is a second stateful service whose failover can strand BullMQ job state, mitigated only by the Postgres grading_job mirror and the 15-minute reconciliation; 'NAT only for the judge service' is inconsistent with every service shipping OTel and Sentry traffic to SaaS endpoints (either a collector gateway with egress or NAT for all is needed); the judge path is up to four calls (screen, judge, narrative, one regeneration) at about $0.17 median against LLM-08's 3-call cap and COST-02's $0.15 median; the third-party pentest is deferred to v1.x against LMS NFR-06 and PLT SEC-01. Every one of these is a configuration, consolidation or scheduling fix rather than an architectural one, which is why it edges the simpler proposal.

**Keel:** Security posture is close to Lockstep's: private subnets with NAT, worker-analysis containers with no egress beyond Postgres/Redis/S3, read-only root FS and non-root user, engine.wasm executed in Node worker_threads with a 1 GB memory cap and 60-second wall clock that can actually be terminated, RLS with a CI presence test and SET LOCAL app.tenant_id/app.actor_id, MFA step-up for /ops, tenant-prefixed Redis keys and S3 prefixes, in-transaction audit outbox with a failed audit write failing the request, WORM audit bucket, per-course daily LLM spend cap plus per-tenant monthly budgets and a global kill switch, pentest and ASVS closure before the first institutional contract. The two-artifact split (engine.wasm and checks.wasm, separately pinned) makes exam-mode 'code checks off' enforceable by construction and lets a checks errata ship without a new engine digest, which is the cleanest answer to EXAM-01 and OPS-05 of the three. Backup/DR matches the guardrail (PITR, 35-day and 1-year snapshots copied to a second region, S3 CRR, rehearsed restore, 60-minute environment rebuild). Deductions: the widest surface (Next.js + Fastify + docs app + three worker services + Redis Streams + a multiplexed WebSocket stream + element/analysis/pack/identity/event-bus registries + CellContext) on the least credible estimate (89 engineer-weeks across 17 steps), so hardening in step 17 is what slips; the WebSocket /v1/stream carries presence, op streaming and job progress with no stated per-subscription authorization or Origin check, a new IDOR and cross-site-hijacking surface over education records; no console 'scale to burst ceiling' control and autoscaling deferred to v1.x, so OPS-12 relies on pre-sized pools (2 analysis tasks x 2 threads = 4 authoritative solves at a time); no secrets-rotation runbook (SEC-05); the same Redis plus single-NAT managed-SPOF profile as Lockstep without its subprocess sandbox or nightly replay; image signing deferred (allowed by the guardrail). Cost control and LLM routing are sound ($0.13 median, Opus for formative at MVP with Sonnet as a configured alternative that bumps grader_version).

Best ideas to graft:
- From Keel: one core, two processes: Make the submission-to-grading-job enqueue transactional in Postgres (pg-boss, or an outbox-driven job insert in the same commit) for the grade lane, keeping Redis only for ephemeral state (rate limits, semaphores, SSE fan-out, flag cache). (Turns the OPS-04 'exactly one active or terminal job per submission version' invariant and the MVP-05 zero-lost-submission exit criterion into a database fact instead of something the 15-minute reconciliation repairs after the fact; removes BullMQ-in-Redis as a loss path during a Redis failover on deadline night.)
- From Keel: one core, two processes: Collapse deployables: one Docker image with two or three entrypoints (web+api, grading+misc worker, judge worker) instead of separate web, api, worker-grading, worker-judge and worker-misc services. (Cuts the on-call surface for a 3-5 person team roughly in half while preserving the one property that matters for isolation (only the judge entrypoint has Anthropic egress); fewer task definitions, IAM roles and deploy steps to freeze inside deadline windows.)
- From Keel: one core, two processes: Merge judge and narrative into one structured-output call so a grade is at most screen + judge + one regeneration. (Meets LLM-08's 3-call cap and brings the median back from about $0.17 to about $0.13 (COST-02 median <= $0.15); one warm cache prefix per task instead of two; one fewer place for a 429/529 to stall the judge lane under burst.)
- From Keel: one core, two processes: Copy daily RDS snapshots (and weekly S3 backup snapshots) to a second AWS account in addition to the second region. (Cross-region replication protects against a regional outage but not against a compromised or mis-scripted primary account deleting backups; a second account with a separate KMS key is the cheapest ransomware and account-compromise control and satisfies AVL-02's 'keys separate from primary storage'.)
- From Keel: one core, two processes: Schedule the third-party pentest before the first institutional pilot, add the optional IP allowlist and 12-hour MFA challenge on /ops, and run ClamAV on presigned uploads before attachments are served to staff. (LMS NFR-06 requires a pentest before the first institutional launch and HECVAT asks for it; the operator console is the highest-value attack surface (OPS risks table); 3 x 20 MB learner attachments are rendered by instructors and TAs, so malware scanning closes an obvious phishing path.)
- From Keel: one core, two processes: Expose one-click audited operator controls for 'scale worker pool to burst ceiling', 'pause batch lane' and 'raise tenant concurrency cap' alongside the bounded autoscaler. (A single course's deadline is the realistic blast radius for a spend spike; pausing judged stages per course keeps one instructor's resubmission storm from exhausting the tenant's monthly budget for every other course.)

(Gives the team a sandbox that still enforces memory and time limits with zero extra binaries, and CI already asserts V8-versus-Wasmtime hash equality so either host produces the same grades.)

Fatal flaws:
- Keel: one core, two processes: SBX-02 (must) is not met as written: the authoritative solve runs in-process in the general-purpose worker with no worker_threads/terminate, fuel or epoch limit, on Fargate tasks with public IPs and unrestricted egress, with no read-only root FS, non-root user or orchestrator-enforced limits stated. A single pathological or adversarial model (SEC-03/SBX-04 threat model) blocks the Node event loop and all 18 processor slots of that task, halving the fixed pool during a deadline burst. Fatal for the pilot unless solves move into terminable worker_threads (or a subprocess), workers move to private subnets with a NAT or endpoint-only egress, and the container runs read-only and non-root.
- Keel: one core, two processes: Authentication design contradiction: Auth.js v5's Credentials provider does not create database sessions (JWT strategy only, unless the session row is hand-rolled), yet the proposal relies on database sessions for SEC-02's 60-second revoke-all, the 12 h / 2 h idle timeouts and MFA enforcement with 'no bypass path'. Shipping as written either silently downgrades to non-revocable JWT sessions or grows unbudgeted custom auth code. Fatal for the institutional pilot unless replaced by an explicit server-side session store (as the other two proposals do) before step 1 closes.
- Keel: one core, two processes: The bridle_ops database role used by the /ops route group implies an RLS-bypassing credential loaded into the same web process that serves learners; SEC-03 allows bypass only for migrations and the two-tenant authorization suite cannot prove a bypass role is never reached by a non-ops route. Must be replaced by per-request SET LOCAL app.tenant_id to the target tenant plus a platform-scoped search index, with console reads still emitting disclosure events.
- Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first): Third-party penetration test deferred to 'annual from v1.x' conflicts with LMS NFR-06 (pentest before the first institutional launch) and PLT SEC-01, and with the HECVAT answers pilot institutions will read; fatal for signing the first institutional pilot unless a pentest is scheduled inside step 16 hardening.
- Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first): Operational surface versus team size: Next.js, Fastify, three worker services, a runner binary, ElastiCache Redis, RDS Proxy, NAT and about ten S3 buckets, delivered in the lowest estimate of the three (105 engineer-weeks). Not fatal to the architecture, but fatal to the on-call rotation of a 3-5 person team during the first deadline window unless the deployables are collapsed (one image, two or three entrypoints) and bucket and service sprawl is trimmed before the pilot; 'NAT only for the judge service' must also be reconciled with OTel/Sentry egress from every service.
- Lockstep: Bridle MVP architecture (determinism-first): Judge path of up to four model calls (screen, judge, narrative, regeneration) at about $0.17 median breaks LLM-08's 3-call cap and COST-02's $0.15 median target and doubles the number of provider calls that can stall under a 529 storm; fixable by merging judge and narrative into one structured-output call.
- Keel: The WebSocket /v1/stream multiplexes presence, op streaming of other users' documents and job progress with no stated per-subscription authorization, tenant scoping or Origin check; this is an IDOR and cross-site WebSocket hijacking surface over FERPA education records that the two-tenant HTTP authorization suite does not cover. Fatal unless every subscribe message is authorized through the same authorize(document, caller) function and Origin is validated, or the stream is replaced by SSE/polling at MVP.
- Keel: Schedule credibility: 89 engineer-weeks for the widest surface (Fastify + Next + docs app + three worker services + Redis Streams + WebSocket layer + two wasm artifacts + registries, CellContext, IdentityProvider and EventBus seams) puts ASVS closure, the pentest and the restore drill in the final step; combined with no burst scale control and autoscaling deferred to v1.x (OPS-12 acceptance requires single console controls for scale, pause lane and raise cap), the first deadline night is under-provisioned and under-hardened unless scope is cut or the estimate is reset to Keel two-process levels.
