# Bridle: Structural-Engineering Simulation LMS, Full Plan

## Context

The repo `Bridle-1` is empty (no commits). The goal is a web-based Learning Management System for structural engineers built on the "Dedicated Simulation" strategy: a 3D modeling workspace with live finite-element analysis, built-in grading and task completion tracking, and an AI grader that scores designs against real-world criteria (building codes, site environment such as wind/seismic/snow/soil, efficiency, constructability, engineering judgment).

The user asked for planning in this order: (1) system design and architecture plus the feature requirements, (2) frontend UI/UX, (3) polish, then (4) a monitoring dashboard artifact that shows the progress and logs of every implementation agent working in parallel. ### A2. MVP in one paragraph

A multi-tenant web app (1 to 3 pilot institutions, single US region) where learners sign in (email/password, Google or Microsoft; accounts by roster import, invite or join code), work through guided lessons and tasks in tiers T1 to T4 plus a reduced T5, model steel beams, trusses, and frames in a 3D workspace (2D mode for introductory work; reinforced-concrete members appear only in Analyze tasks with author-declared effective stiffness), see live linear-static results and AISC 360-22 code checks (stability by the Direct Analysis Method, B6) driven by a scenario (simplified ASCE 7-22 wind, seismic ELF, snow, occupancy loads), run a pre-submission self-check built from the same evidence records the grader will produce, shown at the task's disclosure level (full rows on coach tasks, counts on summative tasks, off in exams), submit a hashed model bundle with a written justification, and receive clause-referenced evidence with failing members highlighted in 3D. Formative tasks release deterministic results immediately with an AI-provisional narrative; summative grades are reviewed by an instructor before release. Instructors set up courses and rosters through a wizard, author tasks from eight starter templates with reference solutions, rubrics from one shared schema, per-learner randomization and a calibration run, review grades evidence-first with bulk accept and override, and export a points-based gradebook (generic plus Canvas- and Moodle-importable CSV). Everything a grade depends on (engine, checks, pack, rubric, prompts, model ids) is versioned and recorded on the grade. "Reduced T5" means planar or 3D frames without diaphragm constraints, lateral loads distributed by tributary width, no accidental torsion, ELF only for regular frames (Table 12.6-1), B2 <= 1.5 and the 12.8.7 theta check. The MVP course is steel analysis and design ("Structural Analysis and Design Fundamentals (Steel)"; the title drops Reinforced Concrete per index C1).

The eight starter templates (all code-checked members are W-shapes; design partners may swap within the TL-02 list at P1-05; CAT-01's three-templates-per-tier acceptance is superseded by this guardrail): 1 T1 simply supported beam under UDL, 2D, Analyze (reactions, V and M, deflection; also the tutorial model); 2 T1 cantilever with point load, 2D, Analyze (envelopes); 3 T2 two-span continuous beam with pattern live load, 2D, Check (DEFL, F2 on a given W-shape); 4 T2 Warren truss, 2D, Analyze (axial signs, zero-force members) with a Design variant on W-shape members (D2, E3); 5 T3 roof truss under snow and wind (the S reference scene), Check (given W-shape design, 0.9D + W uplift, key from the checks library); 6 T3 floor beam with LTB and bracing plus a beam-column, Design (F2 with Lb, E3, H1 with B1); 7 T4 2D portal frame under wind (flat or gabled roof recorded on the template), Design with memo (drift, combinations, B2; the capstone candidate); 8 T4 to reduced-T5 3-storey 2D moment frame under ELF (or 3D braced frame, 2 by 2 bays), Optimize (economy against the auto-sized benchmark, B1/B2, theta).

### A3. Explicitly out of the MVP (v1.x and later)

Reinforced-concrete design checks (ACI pack), shells and slabs, Eurocode and NSCP packs, iterative P-delta and buckling, modal and dynamic analysis, large (L-tier) models and WebGPU, SAML/SCIM/LTI, EU region, quiz types beyond multiple choice, multi-select, true/false and unit-aware numeric, video transcoding, cohort benchmarks and optimizer-generated references, team projects beyond a sketch, full operator console, marketplace, the Diagnose error injector and content, the similarity report (fingerprints are stored), the public self-serve sandbox.

### A4. Grading criteria the system must honor (summary of the structural domain notes)

Strength (demand/capacity per AISC clause), serviceability (deflection and drift limits), stability (AISC Chapter C Direct Analysis Method by default: reduced-stiffness analysis with notional loads, B1/B2 amplification, the 12.8.7 theta check, slenderness advisories, mechanism detection), completeness of load cases and combinations, scenario consistency, economy against a benchmark, constructability and section rationalization, model validity, and engineering judgment from the memo. Strength or stability failures are hard gates that no AI narrative can override.

### A5. Non-functional targets that shape the architecture

Live loop at most 100 ms p95 (S tier) and 300 ms (M tier) on the reference laptop; deterministic server re-analysis as the only grading input; WCAG 2.2 AA with the parity path at MVP; OWASP ASVS L1 plus the L2 authentication, access-control, and data-protection controls; 99.5 % availability with PITR and rehearsed restores; per-tenant LLM counters and a global spend cap; full targets in `docs/requirements/06-non-functional-and-platform-requirements.md`.

## Part B: System design and architecture

### B1. Where it lives

`docs/architecture/` (commit `f032ea1`): `architecture.md` (the architecture Bridle builds, 20 sections, about 15,900 words), `decisions.md` (26 recorded decisions with rationale), `build-sequence.md` (16 steps with dependencies and estimates), `proposals/` (the three independent proposals), `judgments.md` (three judges' scores: Lockstep 190, Keel two-process 185, Keel extensibility 181), `refutations.md` (36 issues from two adversarial reviewers, none fatal), `change-log.md` (what the final changed). The synthesis kept Lockstep's determinism spine and grafted the simplicity proposal's operational shape.

### B2. Shape of the system

- **One Rust core** (crates `bridle-canon`, `bridle-fem`, `bridle-packs`, `bridle-checks`, `bridle-scenario`, `bridle-fingerprint`) compiled to two content-addressed WebAssembly artifacts, `engine.wasm` and `checks.wasm`. The browser runs them in an Engine Worker for the live loop and the self-check; the server runs the identical bytes in `bridle-runner`, a warm Wasmtime process with fuel, wall-clock, and memory limits and no network. A native build serves CI, benchmarks, and the CLI.
- **One Docker image, four entrypoints**: `web` (Next.js 15 UI, `/api/v1` route handlers over a framework-agnostic `packages/api-core`, SSE fan-out via Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY), `worker` (pg-boss processors for grading and analysis, runner pool), `judge` (LLM queue through `packages/grader/judge-client` over the official Anthropic SDK), `system` (outbox relay, projections, op-log rollups, scheduled capacity, cron, email).
- **State**: Postgres 16 (row-level-security tenancy, pg-boss queue so a submission and its grading job are one transaction, append-only events and outbox, op-log segments) and S3 (learner data, artifacts by digest, a WORM audit bucket). No Redis at MVP.
- **Hosting**: AWS us-east-2 with RDS Multi-AZ, S3 with Object Lock, ECS Fargate, CloudFront, KMS, SES, Secrets Manager, all in OpenTofu; Grafana Cloud and Sentry for observability; a hosted status page.
- **Frontend**: Next.js 15 and React 19, react-three-fiber v9 on three.js WebGL2 with instancing and BVH picking, a zustand document store with an invertible op log, IndexedDB autosave, KaTeX for memos.

### B3. Determinism and grading integrity

Canonicalization (`canon/1`) rounds every numeric field to a documented quantum, then canonical JSON, then SHA-256; results are hashed as a binary frame. The engine carries a fresh-equivalence invariant (re-assemble every solve, reuse ordering only on an unchanged graph, reuse a factorization only on byte-identical stiffness), so the browser's incremental solve hashes identically to the server's fresh solve and the submission integrity check is hash equality in the normal case, with a quantized result summary for the tolerance comparison when artifacts differ. Every grade-affecting artifact (engine, checks, pack, clause library, datasets, rubric, prompt, judge schema, grader) is an immutable, content-addressed artifact promoted through a two-person gate; every submission and grade stores its pin set; a determinism corpus runs on five WebAssembly hosts before any engine promotion.

### B4. Live loop

Command bus emits invertible ops; the Engine Worker validates, estimates, solves, and transfers overlays; the scene rebuilds only dirty buffers. Planning budget on the reference laptop: about 100 ms p95 for the S reference scene and 200 ms p95 for the M reference scene (10-storey 6 by 6 bay frame, 1,300 members, 20 combinations); the live band is predicted work at most 2e8 flops, 6,000 DOF, 24 cases and 60 combinations, decided by the same estimate() on both sides; larger Tier-M models run in Run mode with progress (3 s target). The self-check runs the same `checks.wasm` and pack the activity pins, and CI asserts its evidence hash equals the server's for every template.

### B5. Grading pipeline

S0 intake (schema validation, re-hash, limits, locked fields, PII scrub, pin-set freeze, receipt within 2 s) → S1 re-analysis in the runner (result match: exact, within tolerance, or platform fault, never a learner error) → S2 deterministic rules producing clause-cited evidence records and an evidence digest of at most 12k tokens → S3 judge (a `claude-haiku-5-5` screen and one `claude-opus-5-5` judge-plus-narrative call with structured outputs, adaptive thinking at a pinned effort, grounded only in the digest, post-validated by a grounding validator, cached; formative judging through Message Batches) → S4 scoring with gates and the archetype activation matrix → S5 review and release. Deterministic evidence is visible within 15 s to the learner on formative tasks and to reviewers on summative tasks (learners see the grading-status timeline until release); judge latency, cost, and effort are measured on 50 golden grades before any SLO is published (planning basis about $0.25 median and $0.50 p95 per summative grade, $0.12 formative in batch).

### B6. Code packs

`LoadsPack` and `ResistancePack` traits with a bundle loader. `pack_us_asce7_22`: LRFD 2.3.1 and 2.3.6 combinations with the notional-load cases (Ni = 0.002 alpha Yi, sign that increases drift) added to every strength combination by the generator, so the learner's combination diff (ERR-03) never flags them; the ASD 2.4.1 set is generated but not selectable until the ASD resistance minor (Omega factors) ships; simplified MWFRS wind (Kzt = 1, enclosed, roof Cp from Fig. 27.3-1 for flat, low-slope and gable roofs up to 45 degrees); ## Part C: Frontend UI/UX

### C1. Where it lives

### C2. The twelve principles (binding for the frontend)

One shell, five modes (Learn, Teach, Author, Admin, Operate), one route table under `/o/:org`. Evidence before score: every grade number renders from an evidence record with unit, governing combination, and clause, through the same components in the live self-check, the released feedback, and the review screen. Provenance before the number (one ProvenanceLabel; summative grades reach learners only after release). Live means honest: analysis state is always one of Fresh, Solving, Stale, Paused, Unstable, Failed, Offline, in words. Parity is a mode, not a fallback: the Tables layout, command line, result tables, and textual summary cover every action, including required snapshots, and ship with the canvas. Colour is never the only channel; red is reserved for fail, over-utilization, and destructive actions. The task's assistance-and-disclosure profile (coach, summative, exam) decides what is shown; the server decides what is served. Teach at the point of failure (validity messages name the entity and cause and offer an undoable fix). Plain words for first-years. Fixed, learnable layout at 1280 by 720 minimum. Nothing silent (scope, degradation, and exam mode are shell banners). Educational-use framing everywhere: the D1 badge in the workspace status bar and on every export footer; never engineer-of-record, stamped or sealed language.

### C3. Navigation and screens

Top bar with mode and organization switchers, breadcrumb, search opening a command palette, notifications; one banner slot; a per-mode left rail; a context header with entity name, term, scope badge, pinned code pack and units, and entity navigation. ### D1. Purpose

One page the user keeps open while implementation runs. ### D2. Data flow

```
implementation agent ──(1) append JSONL──▶ /Users/johnstephenf.garcia/Dev/Dev/Bridle-1/.bridle/progress/<runId>/<agentId>.jsonl   (durable source of truth, gitignored)
                     ──(2) db_write (MCP)─▶ artifact database, collection "events"   (live feed)
orchestrator (main session) ─ run/task events + sync of any JSONL rows that failed to reach the db
dashboard page ──── claude.use("db") onSnapshot("events") ───▶ derives runs, tasks, agents, logs
                ──── file input: drop a .jsonl ───────────────▶ offline view of verbose logs
```

- The artifact declares `{db: {}, user: {}}` (runtime contract 0.2.96). The page is read-only; only Claude writes, through `mcp__claude-artifacts__db_write`.
- Why a custom page and not the stock "Dashboard" artifact type: that type draws datasets from connector queries or attached files and has no live subscription to the artifact's own database, so it cannot tail agent logs as they arrive. The custom page uses `onSnapshot` on the `events` collection.
- Event-sourced on purpose: agents only ever create new documents (no `ifVersion` reads needed, no write conflicts between parallel agents). The page reduces events into current state.
- Worktree-isolated agents write the JSONL to the absolute path above (the main checkout), so logs from all worktrees land in one place.

### D3. Database layout

Collection `tasks` (seeded once from the roadmap in Part E; doc id = task id, e.g. `P2-03`):

```json
{ "id": "P2-03", "title": "Rust direct-stiffness solver core", "phase": "P2", "workstream": "solver",
  "dependsOn": ["P1-01"], "estimate": "L", "order": 203 }
```

Collection `events` (append-only; doc id = `<ts-ms>-<agentId>-<seq>` so ids sort chronologically):

```json
{ "ts": "2026-11-04T10:15:02Z", "runId": "wf_...", "phase": "P2", "taskId": "P2-03",
  "agentId": "P2-03-solver-core", "agentLabel": "solver core",
  "type": "agent.log", "level": "info", "message": "Assembled global stiffness; running benchmark 3/7",
  "progress": 45, "status": "running", "step": "benchmarks", "data": {} }
```

Event `type` values and who emits them:

| type | emitter | required fields |
|---|---|---|
| `run.start` / `run.end` | orchestrator | runId, phase, message |
| `task.status` | orchestrator or agent | taskId, status ∈ planned, queued, running, blocked, review, done, failed |
| `agent.start` | agent | agentId, taskId, message (plan of attack) |
| `agent.progress` | agent | agentId, progress 0-100, step, message |
| `agent.log` | agent | agentId, level ∈ debug, info, warn, error, message |
| `agent.heartbeat` | agent | agentId (sent at least every 10 min on long tasks) |
| `agent.done` / `agent.fail` | agent | agentId, message (summary; for fail, the error and what was tried), data.files (paths changed) |
| `note` | orchestrator | message (human-readable phase notes) |

Derived state on the page: an agent is **stale** if its last heartbeat/log is older than 10 min and it has no `agent.done`/`agent.fail` (a builder or tester whose long command runs in the background heartbeats while it runs and is not stale); a task's status is the latest `task.status` event, else derived from its agents (any running → running; all done → done; any fail → failed).

Write discipline (from the db tool contract): one `db_write` at a time, up to 50 ops per call, 2 s pause after a multi-op call. Agents batch log lines and flush at step boundaries, not per tool call. The dashboard keeps the latest 3,000 events in memory and shows older history only from dropped JSONL files.

### D4. Agent reporting protocol (goes verbatim into every implementation agent's prompt)

```
REPORTING (mandatory)
You are agent <agentId> working on task <taskId> in run <runId>, phase <phase>.
1. On start: append an agent.start event, then at every step boundary an agent.progress event, and at least one heartbeat/log every 10 minutes. On finish: agent.done (or agent.fail) with a 3-6 sentence summary and the list of files you changed.
2. Each event is one JSON line appended to /Users/johnstephenf.garcia/Dev/Dev/Bridle-1/.bridle/progress/<runId>/<agentId>.jsonl (create the directory if missing). Use `date -u +%Y-%m-%dT%H:%M:%SZ` for ts.
3. Mirror the same events to the monitor: load the tool with ToolSearch "select:mcp__claude-artifacts__db_write" and call it with slug <slug>, ops = one {op:"set", path:"events/<ts-ms>-<agentId>-<seq>", data:{...}} per event (batch up to 50). If the call fails, keep going; the orchestrator syncs the JSONL later.
4. Never block on reporting; never log secrets; keep messages specific (what you did, what you are doing next, what is blocking).
```

A helper script `tools/monitor/log.sh -r <runId> -p <phase> -a <agentId> -t <taskId> -T <type> -m "<message>" [-P <0-100> -s "<step>"] [-l <level>] [-d '<json>']` writes the JSONL line and prints the mirror op so agents do not hand-write JSON; agents in worktrees call it by absolute path. Milestones, each with its proof. M-A hash parity (P0-06, P0-02, P1-01, P1-02, P0-09, P2-01, P2-02.1 to .2): `cargo test --workspace` passes and the golden corpus plus the five closed-form beam benchmarks hash identically in native, Node wasm and Wasmtime (a test-only wasm build; the facade comes with P2-05); no cloud account needed. ## Part F: Verification

Every implementation card carries its own tests (the factory's test and review stations gate every ship: `pnpm check` on every card, `pnpm e2e:smoke` with screenshots attached under `## Test` for cards touching apps/web, and the ship step pushes and records the Actions run). Beyond that, the plan is verified end to end at these levels. Phase exits, run when a phase's last card ships and recorded as a `note` event on the monitor: P0 -> 7; P1 -> the level-1 golden corpus hash equality in native, Node wasm and Wasmtime plus the schema-drift and quanta-walk tests; P2 -> 1 and 2; P3 -> 4; P4 -> the tenancy-isolation tests of 5 and the authentication, MFA and rate-limit flows of 6; P5 -> 3; P6 -> the instructor flow of 5 (offering from template, calibrate, publish); P7 -> 5 end to end, 6, 8 and the launch-readiness acceptance.

1. **Solver and checks core**: a benchmark suite of about 25 problems with closed-form or published worked-example answers (beams, trusses, 2D and 3D frames with releases and member loads, B1/B2 amplification cases), run in CI natively (tolerance 1e-9, informational) and as wasm; hash equality across the five wasm hosts (Node, Wasmtime, Chromium, Firefox, WebKit) on the determinism corpus, not only the 25 problems, gates every engine promotion, and any result outside 1e-6 relative of a worked example fails the build.
2. **Code pack**: clause-level tests for every AISC 360-22 check the MVP cites (about 50 clauses) and for the ASCE 7-22 combination generator and simplified load derivation (the 3-storey wind and 4-storey SMF ELF hand-calc cases, combination golden lists); every applicable W-shape example in AISC Design Examples v16.0 for the MVP limit states (D2, E3, F2/F3, F6, G2, H1; about 15 to 20 cases) within 1 percent on phi Mn and phi Pn, plus SME-authored cases to reach at least 30 reproductions (at least two per limit state, including Cb != 1 segments, Lb between Lp and Lr, noncompact-flange F3 and DAM B1/B2 frames); the reference data (AISC shapes v16) validated against known section properties. This set and the signed SME verification record are the exit gate of P2-07 and P2-08, not a month-9 milestone.
3. **Grading pipeline**: golden submissions per starter template (an oracle that passes, a null submission, and seeded flaws such as a missing load case, a mechanism, an over-utilized member, a wrong unbraced length) with expected evidence and scores; the LLM stage is tested with pinned model ids, structured outputs, and a grounding validator that rejects any number not present in the evidence; a replay job re-grades released grades and must reproduce them.
4. **Workspace**: headless tests of the model editing commands and canonical serialization; a layout test at 1280 by 720 and 1366 by 768 asserting the canvas budget; keyboard-only completion of a T1 task through the Tables layout; a fuel-regression gate in CI and the weekly self-hosted M1/Tiger Lake run of the reference scenes as the R14 latency evidence; the canvas-versus-Tables e2e that builds the benchmark truss both ways and asserts model-hash equality.
5. **LMS and authoring**: end-to-end flows in a browser: instructor creates an offering from a template through the setup wizard in under 30 minutes, calibrates a task, a learner completes and submits it, the grade is reviewed and released, the gradebook exports in the generic and Canvas layouts; a formative task releases deterministic results immediately with the AI-provisional label and its narrative arrives from the batch route; a TA scoped to section A is refused a section B submission; a learner files a regrade and sees the decision and grade history; a new learner completes the onboarding tutorial keyboard-only in the Tables layout; role and tenancy isolation tests (a user from one institution can never read another's data).
6. **Platform**: authentication and MFA flows, rate limits, sandboxed solver jobs with CPU and memory limits, backup restore rehearsal, observability dashboards showing per-tenant LLM spend, accessibility checks (automated plus one screen reader per OS).
7. **The monitor and factory themselves**: `node tools/monitor/test/monitor.test.js` and `python3 tools/monitor/test/check.test.py` pass; every card that ships has a commit named after it, a filled card and a pushed Actions run; the Factory view shows the card at Ship; you look at the rendered UI once per phase close for P3, P4, P5 and P6.
8. **Legal, licensing, and verification**: the licensing register gate runs in CI from P2-06 on (no pack or dataset artifact promotes without an approved register entry, LIC-01); the no-verbatim scan and the disclaimer snapshot tests run on every pack and UI build (TXT-01, DIS-01); the US pack cannot become the default artifact without the signed SME verification record and the published verification report (VER-01, VER-02); the pilot agreement is signed before any institution-managed account is provisioned (TOS-02); ToS/AUP acceptance with terms version is recorded for every account (TOS-01, PRD-02); counsel determinations for register items 1 to 3 and 5 to 8, the DMCA agent, insurance certificates, the practice-act memo, the draft VPAT, HECVAT Lite and the DPIA exist before go-live (D22).

Acceptance for the MVP, in two steps. Launch readiness (2027-12-01): harness gates on the golden sets (exact-level agreement >= 85 percent, within-one-level >= 97 percent, QWK >= 0.80), >= 10 golden submissions per summative task, the course (6 to 8 modules, 15 to 20 activities) assembled and date-shift tested, pentest remediated, WCAG audit, restore drill, k6 burst with the live-judge slice, level 8 complete. MVP validated: the MVP-05 criteria after the spring-2028 pilot term with a design-partner cohort (>= 30 learners, >= 6 tasks, >= 200 reviewed submissions, agreement >= 85 percent within +/-5, override < 15 percent, feedback-usefulness >= 75 percent) plus the instructor-effort measures (PER-06 set-up <= 30 minutes, PER-07 reviewer time <= 3 minutes), per `docs/requirements/08-product-definition-personas-mvp-scope.md`.
