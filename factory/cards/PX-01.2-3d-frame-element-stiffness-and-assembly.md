# PX-01.2 3D frame element stiffness and assembly

## Spec

Pitch card S2 of `docs/plan/pitch-plan.md` (core lane; packages/model and packages/solver), approved by the owner with the plan on 2026-11-18. What: 3D frame element: local stiffness, transformation, assembly. Why: the showcase of 2026-12-16 for the owner's pitch and thesis defense on 2027-01-01. Review station: yes (the full bar: a wrong number or a false statement fails the card). The foreman writes this card's acceptance criteria here before its build starts.

Acceptance criteria (foreman, 2026-11-18):
1. A new workspace package `packages/solver` (`@bridle/solver`, TypeScript, set up like `packages/model`, depending on `@bridle/model` by `workspace:*`, no other runtime dependency) exports `elementStiffnessLocal`: the 12 by 12 stiffness matrix of a straight, prismatic, linear-elastic 3D frame element by Euler-Bernoulli theory (no shear deformation; the README says so), in the member's local axes, with the degrees of freedom ordered ux, uy, uz, rx, ry, rz at the start node and then at the end node. Its inputs are in the model's units (kN, m).
2. `elementTransformation` builds the 12 by 12 transformation from the three local axes that `memberLocalAxes` of `@bridle/model` returns, and `elementStiffnessGlobal` returns T-transposed times k times T.
3. `assemble(model)` returns the global stiffness matrix as a dense square matrix of size six times the number of nodes, and the map from a node id and a degree of freedom to its row, with the nodes in the model's order. It refuses a model that `validateModel` reports issues for, returning those issues. A member of kind `truss` and a member with any end release are refused with a typed "not supported yet" result until card S5; the README says so.
4. Tests, each with a tolerance stated in the test: the local matrix is symmetric; its entries equal the textbook values (EA/L, 12EI/L³, 6EI/L², 4EI/L, 2EI/L, GJ/L) with the right signs for one element with round numbers; for elements in at least five orientations, among them vertical upward, vertical downward, skew in 3D, and one with a roll angle of 30 degrees, the global element matrix times each of the six rigid-body motions is zero; the transformation is orthogonal; the global matrix of a member along global Y equals the local matrix with the axes permuted as the local-axis rule says; two collinear elements assemble to the known sums at the shared node; the assembled matrix of the portal frame fixture is symmetric.
5. The sign conventions (what a positive displacement, rotation, force and moment mean in local and global axes; right-hand rule) are written once in the README and in the source header, and agree with `packages/model`.
6. `pnpm check` is 7/7 and `bash tools/test/lanes.test.sh` passes.
Files: `packages/solver/` (new), `pnpm-lock.yaml`. Out of scope: supports, loads, solving (S3), member loads (S4), releases and truss members (S5).

The mathematics, and where each sign comes from (derived, not copied; the same text is in the header of
`src/element.ts`):
- Convention: translations and forces positive along the positive axis; rotations and moments positive by the
  right-hand rule about the positive axis; the same in local and global axes. The matrix gives the actions of the nodes
  on the member ends, f = k u.
- One bending plane, deflection d(x) and slope s = dd/dx, no load between the ends: d is a cubic, fixed by
  (d1, s1, d2, s2) through the Hermite functions N1 = 1 - 3t^2 + 2t^3, N2 = L(t - 2t^2 + t^3), N3 = 3t^2 - 2t^3,
  N4 = L(-t^2 + t^3), t = x/L. From the strain energy, k_ij = integral of EI N_i'' N_j'' dx, which gives
  EI/L^3 [12, 6L, -12, 6L; 6L, 4L^2, -6L, 2L^2; -12, -6L, 12, -6L; 6L, 2L^2, -6L, 4L^2]. Checked by hand for k_12:
  EI/L^2 times the integral over 0..1 of (-6 + 12t)(-4 + 6t) = 24 - 42 + 24 = 6.
- Slope against rotation: a small rotation theta moves the axis point r = (x, 0, 0) by theta x r =
  (0, theta_z x, -theta_y x). So d(uy)/dx = +rz and d(uz)/dx = -ry. The x-y plane (bending about z, E Iz) uses the
  matrix above as it is on (uy1, rz1, uy2, rz2). In the x-z plane (bending about y, E Iy), rotation = -slope, so on
  (uz1, ry1, uz2, ry2) each deflection-rotation term changes sign and the others do not: uy1-rz1 is +6EIz/L^2 and
  uz1-ry1 is -6EIy/L^2; uy2-rz2 is -6EIz/L^2 and uz2-ry2 is +6EIy/L^2. The code does exactly this: one function
  `planeBending` in (d, s), placed twice with slope signs (+1) and (-1).
- Axial and torsion: linear shape functions give EA/L [1, -1; -1, 1] on (ux1, ux2) and GJ/L [1, -1; -1, 1] on (rx1, rx2).
- Transformation: T is four copies of R on the diagonal, the rows of R being the local x, y, z unit vectors of
  `memberLocalAxes` in global coordinates, so u_local = T u_global; with f_global = T^T f_local, k_global = T^T k T.
- The result has the layout I expect of the usual textbook 12 by 12 matrix (for example McGuire, Gallagher and Ziemian,
  Matrix Structural Analysis), but I had no book open: the reviewer should compare against one. The proofs in this card
  are the tests: the rigid-body test (which a reversed sign in one plane fails; a test shows that) and the cantilever
  formulas P L^3 / 3EI and P L^2 / 2EI in both planes.

Decisions:
- `assemble` returns, besides the matrix and the DOF map of criterion 3, `elements`: per member its id, length, local
  axes, local matrix, transformation and twelve global rows. S3 and S4 need these for member end forces
  (f_local = k T u); returning them avoids computing them twice.
- `DofMap` is plain data (an array, a `Map`, a number) with free functions, so it can cross a Web Worker boundary.
- Refusals are a discriminated union: `{ ok: false, reason: "invalid-model", issues }` and
  `{ ok: false, reason: "not-supported-yet", unsupported: [{ memberId, feature: "truss-member" | "end-release" }] }`.
  Validation is checked first.
- `elementStiffnessLocal` does not check its inputs; `assemble` calls it only after `validateModel` passed.
- The global element matrix is not symmetrized after T^T k T; it is symmetric to rounding (tested at 1e-12 relative),
  exactly symmetric only when T has entries 0 and 1 and -1. S3 should not rely on exact symmetry.

Deviations from the Spec: none. One extra beyond it: the `elements` list above.

For the reviewer and for S3:
- Found in `packages/model` (not changed, reported here): `validateModel` does not check that numbers are finite. An
  infinite node coordinate passes validation (the length is Infinity, which is not below the zero-length tolerance) while
  `memberLocalAxes` returns `undefined`; `assemble` then throws an `Error` (tested). An infinite E or a NaN roll angle
  also passes and would give non-finite matrix entries. `parseModel` input cannot hold such numbers, so this needs a
  model built in code.
- The assembled matrix has no supports applied and is singular; a node with no member has zero rows.
- The README's sentences on theory limits (no shear deformation, no warping, principal axes) are statements about the
  formulation, not tested by code.

Observations (advisory, not part of the verdict):
- For S3 (supports, nodal loads, solve):
  - Reactions are R = K u - f on the restrained rows, with the whole assembled matrix; the check rows c1 show this
    closes equilibrium to 1e-15. Keep the unreduced matrix for that.
  - The matrix is badly scaled by nature: for the fixtures' section the element eigenvalues run from 2GJ/L (4.6) to
    2EA/L (3.2e5), and the free portal from 0.495 to 5.7e5. A zero-pivot or instability test in S3 must be relative to
    the scale of the matrix (a torsion pivot of a few kN·m is legitimate), not an absolute threshold.
  - The assembled matrix is symmetric only to rounding (measured 4.8e-19 of the largest entry on a skew element);
    a Cholesky or LDLᵀ solve should read one triangle or symmetrize first.
  - A node that no member touches has six zero rows, and a model whose members leave a rotation without stiffness
    (later, truss nodes in S5) has zero rotational rows: S3 has to detect and report these, not divide by zero.
  - `multiply` and `addAt` go through the bounds-checked `at` on every entry; fine for 12 by 12, slow as the inner
    loop of a dense solve of a thousand rows. S3 should index `data` directly in its factorization.
  - `assemble` runs `validateModel` on every call; a caller that validated already pays twice (small).
  - Near-vertical members: `memberLocalAxes` switches rule at a horizontal projection of 1e-9 of the length, so a
    column that is out of plumb by more than that gets the "not vertical" axes (local y horizontal, perpendicular to
    its lean), which can turn Iy and Iz by up to 90° against a truly vertical neighbour. That is the rule of
    `packages/model`, correctly followed here; models imported or generated with rounding in coordinates will meet it.
- `packages/model` (not this card's files): the builder's note is confirmed. `validateModel` accepts E = Infinity,
  a NaN or infinite roll angle and an infinite coordinate; it rejects E = NaN (`non-positive-value`) and reports a NaN
  coordinate as `zero-length-member`, which is the wrong name for it. A `non-finite-value` issue in `validateModel`
  would let `assemble` drop its `throw`; until then S3 should not assume finite entries.
- `packages/solver/src/dof.ts:22`: `DofMap.nodeIndex` is a `Map`; it crosses a Web Worker boundary by structured
  clone, as the comment says, but it is lost by `JSON.stringify`. Worth knowing if results are ever saved as JSON.
- The builder's tests of criterion 4 compare the local matrix with a matrix written by the same author; the
  independent comparison is the first row of the table above.
