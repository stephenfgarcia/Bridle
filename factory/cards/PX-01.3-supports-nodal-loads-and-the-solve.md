# PX-01.3 Supports, nodal loads and the solve

## Spec

Pitch card S3 of `docs/plan/pitch-plan.md` (core lane; packages/model and packages/solver), approved by the owner with the plan on 2026-11-18. What: Supports and nodal loads; solve; displacements and reactions; a clear error for an unstable model. Why: the showcase of 2026-12-16 for the owner's pitch and thesis defense on 2027-01-01. Review station: yes (the full bar: a wrong number or a false statement fails the card). The foreman writes this card's acceptance criteria here before its build starts.

Acceptance criteria (foreman, 2026-11-18):
1. `@bridle/solver` exports `solve(model)`. For each load case of the model it applies the supports (the six restraint flags of each `Support`) and the nodal loads of that case, solves the reduced system, and returns the displacements of every node (six components, in m and rad) and the reactions at every support (six components, in kN and kN·m; zero for unrestrained components), keyed by node id. Member loads are not handled yet: a model that has any member load is refused with the typed "not supported yet" result, like truss members and releases, until card S4.
2. Reactions are computed from the unreduced matrix (K u minus the applied nodal loads), not from the reduced system.
3. The linear system is solved by a symmetric factorization written in this package (Cholesky or LDLᵀ on the dense reduced matrix, working on the typed array directly, without the bounds-checked accessors inside the inner loops). No runtime dependency is added.
4. An unstable model is reported, not solved: `solve` returns a typed result `unstable` when the reduced matrix is not positive definite relative to its scale (a pivot below a stated relative threshold), and names the node and the degree of freedom of the first bad pivot. This covers: no support at all (already a validation issue), too few restraints (a rigid-body mechanism), a node that no member connects (zero rows), and a beam left free to spin about its own axis. The threshold and its reason are in the README.
5. `validateModel` of `packages/model` gains a check that every number of the model is finite (coordinates, section and material properties, roll angle, load values and positions), with its own issue code and message key, and no longer reports a NaN coordinate as a zero-length member; the model package's tests cover it. This closes the defect that cards S2's build and review recorded.
6. Tests against closed-form results, each with its tolerance stated in the test: a cantilever with a tip force (deflection, rotation, and the fixed-end reactions including the moment's sign); a cantilever with a tip moment; an axial bar; a propped cantilever or a two-span beam built from two elements with a nodal load at the joint (a statically indeterminate case with a known reaction); the portal frame fixture under its lateral load: the sum of reactions balances the applied load in forces and in moments about the origin, and the sway agrees with the slope-deflection value when the axial stiffness is made large; a 3D case: a cantilever column with tip forces in both horizontal directions and a torque. Each unstable case of criterion 4 has a test. Two load cases in one model give two independent results.
7. The result type is plain data (objects, arrays, typed arrays) so that it can cross a Web Worker boundary, and the README documents it, the units and the signs of displacements and reactions.
8. `pnpm check` is 7/7 and `bash tools/test/lanes.test.sh` passes.
Files: `packages/solver/`, `packages/model/` (criterion 5 only), `pnpm-lock.yaml` if needed. Out of scope: member loads and member end forces (S4), releases and truss members (S5), values along a member (S6), the worker (S9).

### The algorithm

1. `assemble(model)` gives K (6 rows per node, no supports). Its refusals are passed on. Any entry of
   `memberUniformLoads` or `memberPointLoads`, in any load case, makes the result `not-supported-yet`, with one
   `{ loadId, memberId, feature: "member-load" }` per load after the entries for truss members and releases.
2. A degree of freedom whose support flag is true has the displacement 0. The reduced matrix is K without those rows and
   columns, copied by direct indexing.
3. `factorize` makes the reduced matrix exactly symmetric by averaging each pair of mirrored entries into the lower
   triangle (the foreman's first option), then computes L D Lᵀ row by row without pivoting. The inner loops index the
   `Float64Array` directly. Each row starts at its first nonzero column (the profile), which the factor keeps, so the
   cost depends on the bandwidth that the node order gives, although the storage is dense.
4. Per load case: f is the sum of the case's nodal loads per degree of freedom; u solves the reduced system; the
   reactions are the restrained rows of K u − f with the whole matrix as assembled (criterion 2). A load on a restrained
   degree of freedom therefore appears in the reaction with the opposite sign.
5. Per load case: the sensitivity check (below).

### The instability rule, and why

A pivot d of a degree of freedom is accepted when d > 1e-9 · k, k being that degree of freedom's own diagonal entry of
K. The first pivot that fails, in the model's node order (ux to rz within a node), gives
`{ ok: false, reason: "unstable", nodeId, dof, pivot, diagonal, threshold }`. The reduced index is mapped back through
the list of free rows to a node id and one of ux, uy, uz, rx, ry, rz.

- Why relative to the own diagonal and not to the largest entry (the foreman's example): translations and rotations have
  different units, so the largest diagonal entry (an axial E A/L in kN/m) is not comparable with a rotational pivot
  (kN·m). The ratio d/k has no unit, does not change with the units, and is what loses digits: d is formed by subtracting
  non-negative terms from k.
- Why 1e-9: it sits between what I measured on the two sides with the fixtures' section. Mechanisms: 0 exactly for
  axis-aligned cases; |d|/k of 2e-16 to 2e-14 for one skew member and the pinned portal; up to 1.3e-11 for space frames
  of 96 and 126 members (rotation about a line of pinned bases, and a frame on one pin and two rollers); 2e-13 for a
  200-element skew chain free to spin. Stable models: the smallest ratio was 4.9e-8 (a 200-element skew cantilever
  numbered from the root), 2.7e-6 and 2.7e-8 for the portal with the area times 1e4 and 1e6, 5e-4 and more for the space
  frames. 1e-10 was my first value; it left only a factor of 8 above the 1.3e-11 measured for a mechanism, so I moved to
  1e-9 (a factor of 77 above it and a factor of 49 below the stable 4.9e-8).

What the rule cannot do, found while testing and covered by tests and by the README:
- It reports a stable model as unstable when stiffness terms differ too much: the portal fixture with the area times
  1e8 (ratio 2.7e-10 at n3 ux).
- It can pass a mechanism. The rounding left in a mechanism's pivot grows with the stiffness contrast: for the 96-member
  space frame pinned along one line the ratio is 3.5e-12 with the fixture's area, -3.5e-9 with the area times 100 (caught
  because it is negative), -5.3e-8 times 1e4 (the same), and positive and above the threshold with the area times 1e5.
  That model came out "solved" with a sway of 118 km. No fixed pivot threshold separates these cases, so I added a
  second check, which the Spec did not ask for.

### The sensitivity check (an addition to the Spec)

For each load case, sensitivity = ε · Σ|u_i||K_ij||u_j| / Σ f_i u_i, with ε = `Number.EPSILON`. It is the relative change
of the loads' work that a relative change of ε in every entry of K could cause, for the displacements found. Above 1e-3
the call returns `{ ok: false, reason: "ill-conditioned", loadCaseId, sensitivity, limit }`. Measured: ε for an axial
bar, 13 ε for a cantilever, at most 1.4e-5 for the stable models I tried (the worst: the space frame with the area times
1e6, and the 200-element cantilever at 1.2e-5), and 2.6 and 5.4 for the two space frames that passed the pivot test as
mechanisms. The value is in every `LoadCaseResult` as `sensitivity`, so the interface can show it. Its limit: it sees
only mechanisms that a load of the case sets in motion, and nothing in a model without load cases.

### Decisions

- Results are keyed objects (`loadCases[id]`, `displacements[nodeId]`, `reactions[nodeId]`), built with
  `Object.fromEntries` so an id such as `__proto__` is an ordinary key (tested). Each load case also has the two vectors
  `displacementVector` and `reactionVector` (`Float64Array`, six per node in the order of `nodeIds`), which S4 needs for
  member end forces and the interface for the deflected shape. No `Map` in the result.
- `reactions` has an entry for every support, also one that restrains nothing; an unrestrained component is exactly 0
  (it is not computed).
- One more typed refusal, `non-finite`: finite inputs can overflow (E·A = 1e300 · 1e10), which the finite check of
  `validateModel` cannot see. `solve` scans K once, and u and the reactions per load case.
- `validateModel`: a number that is not finite gets `non-finite-value` only (not also `non-positive-value`,
  `zero-length-member` or `load-position-outside-member`). Checked: 24 numbers (node x, y, z; member rollAngleDeg; section
  A, Iy, Iz, J, depthY, depthZ; material E, G, fy, density; nodal load fx to mz; uniform load value; point load value and
  position). A member between nodes with finite coordinates whose length overflows is reported with the same code on the
  member. With that, the `throw` in `assemble` is no longer reached by a model that `validateModel` accepts; I kept it as
  a guard and changed its comment.
- A model with no load case is still factorized, so it is still checked for instability.

### For the reviewer

- The README sentences about measured values are each asserted by a test ("The instability test" and "The sensitivity
  check" list them). The measurements in this section that are not in the README were made with temporary scripts, which
  I deleted; they are not tests.
- The node and degree of freedom of `unstable` is where the factorization stopped, which is the last degree of freedom of
  the mechanism in the node order, not a diagnosis of the cause. The README says so.
- Whether a mechanism near the threshold comes out as `unstable` or `ill-conditioned` depends on rounding in
  `Math.sin`, `Math.cos` and `Math.hypot`; the test of the space frame with the area times 1e5 expects `ill-conditioned`
  and was run on Node 22 only.
- Speed, measured once in vitest on the development machine: a 210-member, 99-node space frame solves in 4.2 ms (median
  of 20 calls; `assemble` is 2.2 ms of it); the 200-element cantilever test takes about 26 ms.

### For S4 (member loads, member end forces) and S9 (the worker)

- S4: remove the member-load refusal in `solve` (`UnsupportedMemberLoad`) and add the equivalent nodal loads to f before
  the solve. The reactions must stay K u − f with that full f, including the parts on restrained rows, and the
  sensitivity uses the same f. `assemble` already returns `elements` (kLocal, transformation, dofIndices); `solve` does
  not return them yet. A member's end forces are kLocal · T · u(dofIndices) minus the fixed-end forces, with u from
  `displacementVector`.
- S9: a result survives `structuredClone` (tested); the two `Float64Array`s per load case can be transferred. `solve` is
  synchronous and keeps no state. Memory is dense: K, the reduced copy and the factor are each 8 · (6n)² bytes. The time
  depends on the node order through the profile. `assemble` runs `validateModel` on every call.

#### Rework loop 1

After the review's FAIL (2026-11-18). The review's one finding is fixed; nothing else of the solve changed. Where this
section and the text above disagree (the rule, the threshold 1e-9, the profile, the sensitivity check, the speed), this
section is the present state.

What changed:
- `packages/solver/src/factor.ts`: `factorize` is now L D Lᵀ **with symmetric pivoting**, P A Pᵀ = L D Lᵀ. I chose the
  factorization itself with a permutation, not a separate check before a profile factorization: one elimination instead
  of two. At each step it eliminates, of the rows that are left, the one with the largest ratio of its current pivot to
  its original diagonal entry (the lowest row among equals), and it stops when that largest ratio is not above the
  threshold. The rows and columns are exchanged in the lower triangle; a step updates only the rows that have a nonzero
  in the eliminated column. `Factorization` now has `order` (the row of A eliminated at each step) and no `firstColumn`;
  the profile of the first build is gone. `solveFactorized` permutes b, solves with L, D, Lᵀ and permutes back. A failed
  factorization also returns `remaining`.
- `packages/solver/src/solve.ts`: `PIVOT_THRESHOLD` is 1e-12 (was 1e-9). The `unstable` result has one more field,
  `remaining`. The result `ill-conditioned` and `SENSITIVITY_LIMIT` are removed; `sensitivity` stays in each
  `LoadCaseResult` as a reported number.
- `packages/solver/src/solve.test.ts`: 59 tests (was 41). `packages/solver/README.md`: the sections "The factorization",
  "The instability test" and "The sensitivity of a result" (was "The sensitivity check") are rewritten, and the list of
  results of `solve`. `packages/solver/src/index.ts`: `SENSITIVITY_LIMIT` is no longer exported.
