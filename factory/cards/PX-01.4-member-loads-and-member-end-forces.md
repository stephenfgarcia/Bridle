# PX-01.4 Member loads and member end forces

## Spec

Pitch card S4 of `docs/plan/pitch-plan.md` (core lane; packages/model and packages/solver), approved by the owner with the plan on 2026-11-18. What: Member loads (uniform and point) with fixed-end forces; member end forces. Why: the showcase of 2026-12-16 for the owner's pitch and thesis defense on 2027-01-01. Review station: yes (the full bar: a wrong number or a false statement fails the card). The foreman writes this card's acceptance criteria here before its build starts.

Acceptance criteria (foreman, 2026-11-18):
1. `solve` accepts member loads: a `MemberUniformLoad` over the whole member and a `MemberPointLoad` at a position along it, each in local or global axes and in the direction x, y or z as `packages/model` defines them (a uniform load is per unit of the member's own length, also for a global direction on an inclined member; a point load's position is in metres from the start node). The refusal of member loads from card S3 is removed.
2. Each member load is turned into equivalent nodal loads through the fixed-end forces of a member fixed at both ends, derived for the Euler-Bernoulli element: for a transverse uniform load w the end shears wL/2 and the end moments wL²/12; for a transverse point load P at a from the start (b = L − a) the shears Pb²(3a + b)/L³ and Pa²(a + 3b)/L³ and the moments Pab²/L² and Pa²b/L²; for an axial uniform or point load the corresponding axial end forces. The signs follow the package's convention (the README states them), and a load given in global axes is resolved into the member's local axes first.
3. Reactions stay K u minus the full load vector, including the parts of the equivalent nodal loads that fall on restrained degrees of freedom, so that a load on a member between two supports appears in the reactions.
4. The result gains, per load case and member, the member end forces in local axes: at each end the axial force, the two shears, the torque and the two bending moments, computed as kLocal · T · u minus the equivalent nodal loads of that member's loads, in the package's sign convention for end actions, with the convention stated in the README (what a positive value means at the start and at the end).
5. The result also carries the smallest pivot ratio of the factorization, so that the interface can warn when a result is close to the stability threshold; the README says what the number means.
6. Tests against closed-form results, each with its tolerance stated in the test: a simply supported beam under a uniform load (reactions wL/2, midspan deflection 5wL⁴/384EI using a node at midspan, end forces); a fixed-fixed beam under a uniform load (end moments wL²/12 with their signs, zero nodal displacements); a cantilever under a uniform load (tip deflection wL⁴/8EI, fixed-end moment wL²/2) and under a point load at an interior position (tip deflection Pa²(3L − a)/6EI); a propped cantilever under a uniform load (reactions 5wL/8 and 3wL/8, moment wL²/8); a fixed-fixed beam with an off-centre point load (end moments Pab²/L² and Pa²b/L²); an inclined member with a global vertical uniform load (total reaction equals w times the member's length); an axial uniform load on a bar; the simply supported beam fixture of `packages/model` under its own uniform load; member end forces in equilibrium with the member's loads for every member of the portal frame fixture with an added beam load; both bending planes and a member with a roll angle.
7. The result stays plain data that survives `structuredClone`; `pnpm check` is 7/7 and `bash tools/test/lanes.test.sh` passes.
Files: `packages/solver/`. Out of scope: releases and truss members (S5), values along a member and diagrams (S6), partial-length distributed loads, moments applied along a member, load combinations.

### The derivation and the sign reasoning

Not copied from a table. The equivalent nodal loads are the consistent nodal loads, Q_i = ∫ q N_i dx, with N_i the
element's own shape functions (the Hermite cubics of `element.ts` for a transverse load, the linear functions for an
axial load). The header of `memberLoads.ts` shows why these are the fixed-end forces with the opposite sign, exactly:
by Betti's theorem between "unit end displacement i, no load" (whose deflected shape is N_i, the exact shape of an
unloaded Euler-Bernoulli member) and "both ends fixed, load q", F_i · 1 + ∫ q N_i dx = 0.

- Weights for a uniform load: ∫N1 = L/2, ∫N2 = L²/12, ∫N3 = L/2, ∫N4 = −L²/12; axial L/2 and L/2.
- Weights for a point load at a (b = L − a): N1(a) = b²(3a + b)/L³, N2(a) = ab²/L², N3(a) = a²(a + 3b)/L³,
  N4(a) = −a²b/L²; axial b/L and a/L. These are the values of criterion 2.
- N2 and N4 are conjugate to the slope. In the local x-y plane the slope is +rz, so the moment about z is +Q: a load
  along +y gives +wL²/12 at the start and −wL²/12 at the end. In the local x-z plane the slope is −ry, so the moment
  about y is −Q: a load along +z gives −wL²/12 at the start and +wL²/12 at the end. Check by statics: a force along +z
  at x > 0 has the moment (x, 0, 0) × (0, 0, P) = (0, −xP, 0) about the start.
- A global load is a vector along the global axis; its local components are its dot products with the local axes.
  A uniform load stays per unit of the member's length.
- `solve` adds Tᵀ Q to f at the member's twelve rows, and the end forces are k · (T · u) − Q in local axes, Q being the
  sum over the member's loads of the load case.

The sign convention of the reported end forces is the package's convention for f = k u: what the node applies to the
member end, in local axes, positive along and about the positive local axes at both ends. Tension N with no axial
load along the member is `start.fx` = −N, `end.fx` = +N. Worked example (README, and a test): cantilever of 3 m along
+X fixed at its start, 10 kN downward on the tip node: start `fz` = +10, `my` = −30; end `fz` = −10, `my` = 0.

### Decisions

- `smallestPivotRatio` is at the top of the result, not per load case: the factorization is made once per model. It is
  computed in `solve` from the factorization's `pivots` and `order` and the diagonal of the reduced matrix; `factor.ts`
  is not changed. It is 1 when no degree of freedom is free (documented and tested).
- `memberEndForces` is a record of plain objects by member id (built with `Object.fromEntries`, so the id `__proto__`
  is an ordinary key; tested), and `memberIds` gives the model's order. There is no `Float64Array` of end forces.
- A point load's position below 0 or beyond the length is taken as the end (`validateModel` allows 1e-9 m of slack).
- A zero component is written as +0, never −0 (`+ 0` in two places of `memberLoads.ts`), so that results compare equal
  with `toEqual`.
- The point-load weights are computed from a and b = L − a as products, so a load 1 mm from an end keeps its digits
  (tested at relative 1e-12 through `solve`).

### For S5 (releases and truss members) and S6 (values along a member)

- S5: the equivalent nodal loads here are those of a member fixed at both ends. With an end release the fixed-end
  forces change (a released moment must come out zero in f = k u − Q), so Q has to be condensed together with k: for
  the released degrees of freedom r and the kept ones c, k* = k_cc − k_cr k_rr⁻¹ k_rc and Q* = Q_c − k_cr k_rr⁻¹ Q_r.
  This is a suggestion, not something this card built or tested. For a truss member S5 must decide what a transverse
  member load means; `solve` adds Tᵀ Q for every member without looking at its kind.
- S5: `solve` passes on the refusal of `assemble` unchanged, so it needs no change when `assemble` stops refusing.
- S6: the internal forces along a member follow by statics from `memberEndForces[id].start` and the member's loads,
  resolved with `memberLoadLocalComponents`. The signs must be derived once from this convention: the same state has
  opposite signs seen as a start and as an end. A sagging moment under a downward load on a beam along +X with roll 0
  is `my` = +M as a start and −M as an end; in the x-y plane, under a load along −y, it is `mz` = −M as a start and +M
  as an end (both tested). The result does not carry the loads; the model does.
- Both: `memberEndForces` is per load case and has an entry for every member, also for unloaded ones.

### Observations (advisory, not part of the verdict)

For S6 (values along a member, diagrams):
- The end forces are node-on-member actions in local axes, not internal forces. From the start actions and the
  member's local load components the internal bending moment follows by statics of the piece [0, x], and the two
  planes differ in the sign of the shear term: about y, (my at a cut end at x) = −my₁ − x·fz₁ − Σ pz·(x − ξ);
  about z, (mz at a cut end at x) = −mz₁ + x·fy₁ + Σ py·(x − ξ). The sagging moment of a beam along +X with roll 0
  is M(x) = my₁ + fz₁·x + Σ pz·(x − ξ) (check: 0 + 30·3 − 10·3²/2 = 45). Derive each diagram sign once from this and
  test it in both planes; copying the x-z rule to the x-y plane gives the wrong sign.
- "Sagging" has a meaning only for a beam with roll 0. On a column or a rolled member draw the moment on the
  tension side by the local axis (+M about y puts tension on the −z fibre for a start-side sign, and so on), and
  state the rule in the interface. A column running downward has local z = +X, an upward one −X, so the same wind
  gives opposite signs of the local values.
- Axial: N (tension positive) = −`start.fx` − Σ px up to x, and = +`end.fx` at the end. Shear likewise changes sign
  between the start action and the usual internal shear.
- A point load at position 0 or L is on the member, not on the node: the end action there does not include it
  (case F: the end force differs from the nodal-load version by exactly the load). The diagram has its jump at the
  very end, and the value "at the node" depends on the side.
- A position outside [0, L] within the 1e-9 m slack is taken as the end by `equivalentNodalLoadsLocal`; S6 must
  clamp the same way. A uniform load is per unit of member length also for a global direction; S6 must resolve
  it with `memberLoadLocalComponents` and not project it.
- The mid-span values are exact at the nodes; along a member S6 has to add the fixed-end (particular) part of the
  deflection to the cubic of the end displacements, or the deflection of a loaded member is wrong between nodes
  (the end values and the internal forces by statics are exact).

For S5 (releases, truss members):
- Q is that of a member fixed at both ends. With a release the released moment must come out zero in k·T·u − Q, so
  Q must be condensed with k; I agree with the Build section's formula, which this card does not test. A test for
  S5: a propped cantilever made with a release under a uniform load (5wL/8, 3wL/8, wL²/8) against the same made
  with a support, and member equilibrium with my check above.
- A truss member has no bending stiffness: a transverse member load on it must either be refused or be carried to
  the nodes as simple-beam reactions (wL/2 each, no moments); adding Tᵀ Q with the fixed-end moments would put
  moments on nodes that may have no rotational stiffness. Today `assemble` refuses the model first.
