# PX-01.6 Values along a member

## Spec

Pitch card S6 of `docs/plan/pitch-plan.md` (core lane; packages/model and packages/solver), approved by the owner with the plan on 2026-11-18. What: Values along a member: axial, shear, moment, deflected shape. Why: the showcase of 2026-12-16 for the owner's pitch and thesis defense on 2027-01-01. Review station: yes (the full bar: a wrong number or a false statement fails the card). The foreman writes this card's acceptance criteria here before its build starts.

Acceptance criteria (foreman, 2026-11-18):
1. `@bridle/solver` exports a function that, given a model, a solve result, a load case id, a member id and a number of stations (default 21, ends included), returns the member's internal actions and deflections along its length: at each station the distance from the start node, the axial force N, the shear forces Vy and Vz, the torque T, the bending moments My and Mz, and the deflection of the member's axis as three components in global axes and in local axes. It also returns the extreme values of each quantity with their positions, where the positions of extreme moments are found exactly (at the stations, at point loads and where the shear changes sign), not only among the stations.
2. Internal actions are computed by statics from the member's start end forces and its loads, so they are exact for the loads the solver supports; stations are added at each point load so that the jump in shear appears (two values at the same position, just before and just after the load).
3. Sign convention for an engineer: the function reports internal actions in one stated convention and the README defines it with a figure in words and a table: axial force positive in tension; for each bending plane, what a positive moment and a positive shear mean in terms of the member's local axes (which fibre is in tension), consistently along the member (the same state has the same sign whether it is seen from the start or the end, unlike the end forces); and it says how this relates to "sagging positive" for a horizontal beam with roll 0 under gravity (a simply supported beam under a downward load must report a positive, sagging, moment of wL²/8 at mid-span, in the plane that gravity bends) and what happens for columns and rolled members, where sagging has no meaning.
4. Deflections between the nodes are the element's cubic (and linear, for axial and torsional parts) interpolation of the end displacements plus the particular part of the loads on a member fixed at both ends, so that the mid-span deflection of a single-element simply supported beam under a uniform load equals 5wL⁴/384EI without a node at mid-span.
5. A function returns the deflected shape of the whole structure for drawing: for every member a polyline of points (original position plus scale times deflection) at a given number of stations.
6. Tests against closed-form results, each with its tolerance stated in the test: simply supported beam under a uniform load (M at mid-span and quarter points, V at the ends, deflection at mid-span and quarter points) with one element; the same in the other bending plane; cantilever under a uniform load and under a tip load (M, V and deflection along the length); fixed-fixed beam under a uniform load (hogging at the ends, sagging at mid-span, points of contraflexure at 0.2113 L from each end); a beam with an off-centre point load (the shear jump, the maximum moment under the load, the maximum deflection position at sqrt((L² − b²)/3) from the far end for a simply supported beam); an inclined member with a global vertical load (axial force varying along the length); a column under a horizontal load; a member with a roll angle of 90 degrees; the portal frame fixture with a beam load: moment continuity around each rigid joint, and every station of every member in equilibrium with the end forces. A test proves that N, V, M at the two ends agree with the end forces after the sign conversion.
7. The functions are pure, plain data in and out, usable in the Web Worker and in the main thread; `pnpm check` is 7/7 and `bash tools/test/lanes.test.sh` passes.
Files: `packages/solver/`. Out of scope: drawing (W9, W10), stresses and checks (S8), releases and truss members (S5).

### The convention, chosen before coding

Cut the member at the distance x from its start node; "the part before the cut" is the piece from the start node to
the cut. Everything is in the member's local axes.

| Value | Positive when |
|---|---|
| `N` | tension |
| `Vy`, `Vz` | the forces on the part before the cut (start end force and loads up to the cut) sum to a force along +y, +z |
| `T` | the part beyond the cut applies to the part before it a moment about +x (right-hand rule) |
| `My` (x-z plane, E·Iy) | the fibre on the −z side is in tension |
| `Mz` (x-y plane, E·Iz) | the fibre on the −y side is in tension |

Both planes then read alike: M = EI·d″, V = dM/dx, dV/dx = p, with d and p along the plane's transverse axis (z for
`My`, y for `Mz`). To draw a diagram on the tension side: the ordinate of `My` is the vector −`My` · (local z), that of
`Mz` is −`Mz` · (local y). For a member that is not vertical and has roll 0, local z points upward, so a positive `My`
is sagging.

Derivation (header of `memberValues.ts`). The free body is the part before the cut: the start end force F1, M1 of
`solve` (what the node applies to the member), the loads p at ξ between 0 and x, and at the cut the force R and the
moment C that the part beyond applies. Forces: F1 + Σp + R = 0. Moments about the cut: M1 + (−x, 0, 0) × F1 +
Σ(ξ − x, 0, 0) × p + C = 0, with (−x, 0, 0) × (fx, fy, fz) = (0, x·fz, −x·fy). So Cy = −my₁ − x·fz₁ − Σpz·(x − ξ) and
Cz = −mz₁ + x·fy₁ + Σpy·(x − ξ): the shear term has opposite signs in the two planes, as the review of PX-01.4 said.
A stress σ on the cut face gives C = ∫(0, y, z) × (σ, 0, 0) dA = (0, ∫zσ dA, −∫yσ dA): positive Cy is tension on the +z
side, positive Cz is tension on the −y side. Reported:

    N  = Rx  = −fx₁ − Σpx                     T  = Cx  = −mx₁
    Vy = −Ry = fy₁ + Σpy                      Vz = −Rz = fz₁ + Σpz
    My = −Cy = my₁ + x·fz₁ + Σpz·(x − ξ)      Mz = +Cz = −mz₁ + x·fy₁ + Σpy·(x − ξ)

At the start: N = −fx₁, Vy = fy₁, Vz = fz₁, T = −mx₁, My = my₁, Mz = −mz₁. At the end: N = fx₂, Vy = −fy₂, Vz = −fz₂,
T = mx₂, My = −my₂, Mz = mz₂ (tested on skew rolled members).

Worked example: the simply supported beam fixture (6 m along +X, 10 kN/m down): `start.fz` = +30, `start.my` = 0,
pz = −10. At x = 3: `Vz` = 30 − 30 = 0, `My` = 0 + 3·30 − 10·9/2 = +45 kN·m (sagging, positive).

### Decisions

- Loads are resolved with `memberLoadLocalComponents` and a point load's position is clamped to [0, L], as
  `equivalentNodalLoadsLocal` does. Point loads at the same position are added together.
- Stations: `stations` equally spaced ones, the positions of `at`, and two stations with the same `x` at every
  point-load position (before, after). Positions that are equal as numbers are merged; positions that differ by
  rounding are not. A point load at position 0 or L gives two stations at that end; the one on the node's side equals
  the end force by the table above.
- The sixth parameter `at` (extra positions) is an addition to criterion 1. It is what the test of the points of
  contraflexure uses (0.2113 L is not a station), and what a probe in the interface can use.
- Deflection = the element's interpolation of the end displacements (cubics with the slopes +rz and −ry, linear along
  x) plus the deflection of the member fixed at both ends under its loads (closed-form polynomials, in the header and
  in the README). The second station at a point load copies the deflection of the first, so the two are the same numbers.
- Extremes. N, Vy, Vz, T: over the stations (they are linear between point-load positions and ends, which are
  stations). My, Mz: also where the linear shear between two consecutive stations is zero, by one division; the moment
  there is M − V²/(2w). Deflection components (three local, three global): also, for each pair of consecutive
  stations, the point where the component's derivative changes sign when it has opposite signs at the two ends of the
  pair, by bisection of the derivative computed from the formulas; a derivative at a station of at most 1e-12 times
  its largest size over the member's stations (exactly zero at a fixed end) is replaced by the derivative at 2⁻²⁰ of
  the pair's length inside the pair (rework loop 1). Stated limits: an even number of sign changes within a pair is
  not found, nor a sign change within that 2⁻²⁰ next to such a station.
- A member of kind `truss` or with an end release: `memberValues` returns `undefined`; `deflectedShape` draws the
  straight line between its displaced ends. `solve` refuses such models today, so this is reached only with a result
  that does not belong to the model (the tests do that on purpose). Reason: the fixed-end deflection formulas would be
  wrong for such a member once S5 lets them through.
- The result's `axes` are the member's local axes, so the interface can draw on the tension side without computing
  them again.
- A zero is written as +0, never −0, in the arrays.
- `deflectedShape` takes the undeformed point as (1 − t) · start + t · end, so the first and the last are exactly the
  nodes' positions before the deflection is added.

### For W9, W10, S8 and S5

- W9 (deflected shape and reactions): `deflectedShape(model, result, loadCaseId, scale, stations)`; `points` is flat,
  point s of member m at 3·(m·stations + s), members in the model's order (`memberIds`). The curve includes the
  member's own loads, so one member per beam is enough. No extra points at point loads. It returns `undefined` for a
  failed solve. The largest deflection of a member for a label: `memberValues(...).extremes.dGlobalZ` (or the local
  ones), with its position.
- W10 (diagrams with probes): plot `x` against `N`, `Vy`, `Vz`, `T`, `My`, `Mz`; draw two points at a repeated `x`
  (the jump). On the structure, the ordinate of `My` is −`My` · `axes.z` and of `Mz` is −`Mz` · `axes.y`: that is the
  tension side for every member. Use "sagging/hogging" only where `axes.z.z` > 0. A probe at any position: pass it in
  `at` and read the station with that `x`. Labels of maxima: `extremes`.
- S8 (illustrative checks): the stress at a fibre (y, z) of the section, tension positive, is
  σ = N/A − My·z/Iy − Mz·y/Iz with these signs (from σ = −E·z·uz″ and My = E·Iy·uz″; the same in y). The extreme
  fibres are at z = ±depthZ/2 and y = ±depthY/2. The largest |My| and |Mz| along a member are in `extremes`, exact for
  the loads the solver has; they need not occur at the same station, so a combined stress should be evaluated per
  station (and at `extremes.My.maxX`, `minX` through `at`). Deflection for a limit check: `extremes.dLocalY`,
  `dLocalZ` are total deflections of the axis in local axes, not relative to the chord between the displaced ends;
  S8 must subtract the chord if it wants the sag of a member whose ends move.
- S5 (releases, truss members): `memberValues` returns `undefined` and `deflectedShape` draws a straight chord for a
  truss member or a member with a release. The internal actions by statics stay valid for such members as long as the
  end forces are right; the deflection needs the fixed-end part of a member with that release in place of the
  formulas here. S5 has to lift the guard (`hasRelease`, `member.kind !== "frame"`) and supply that part.

### Observations (do not fail the card)

- W10 (diagrams): −`My`·`axes.z` and −`Mz`·`axes.y` put every diagram on the tension face in all my cases, also for
  rolled columns, so the two ordinates can be drawn as they are. For a horizontal beam the sign of `Mz` under the
  same lateral load flips with the member's direction (local y flips; case (a), second span): labels for `Mz`
  should not say sagging or hogging. At a point load the two stations share `x`; a polyline through the arrays
  draws the jump of the shear correctly.
- W9 (deflected shape): joints are continuous and unkinked to rounding at scale 250. With the default 21 points a
  member's curve is a polygon whose end segments deviate from the joint's tangent by about L/40 times the end
  curvature times the scale; visible only at very large scales. `deflectedShape` adds no points at point loads,
  which is right (no kink of the axis there). It returns `undefined` for the whole structure when one member
  cannot be computed.
- S8 (illustrative checks): the stress formula of the Build section has the right signs. `extremes.dLocal*` are
  total deflections, not relative to the chord, as the Build section says; for a sway frame the difference matters.
  After the fix of finding 1 an extreme next to a fixed end is found too; until then it is short by at most the
  bump described there.
- S5 (releases, truss members): the statics of the internal actions need no change; the guard at
  `memberValues.ts:590` and the chord in `deflectedShape` are the two places to lift, and the fixed-end deflection
  formulas are the part that a release changes.
- `stations` has no upper bound: a very large number allocates that many values. The interface should cap what it
  passes.
- `memberValues` scans the model's lists on each call (stated in the README); for many members per frame the
  interface should call it only for the members it shows, or the function could take the indexed lookup that
  `deflectedShape` already builds.

Checked and found fine: the derivation and every formula of the code, the sign convention against the physics of
beams, cantilevers, columns and a portal frame, the deflected shape at rigid joints, the moment extremes, the edge
cases, the seven acceptance criteria and both commands; the three findings above are the only defects.
